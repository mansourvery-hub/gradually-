# 渐入 (JianRu) — Knowledgeable Story Blueprint V2

> **Status:** Conceptual blueprint / human-facing design document  
> **Purpose:** Define the updated vision for JianRu's expandable-story content system after the discussion on LingQ-inspired progression, narrative continuity, and the "story manifold" concept.

---

## 1. Core Vision

JianRu should not merely present a sequence of increasingly difficult Chinese stories.

Its distinctive content model is:

> **One meaningful story-world gradually becomes more legible as the learner becomes more capable in Chinese.**

A learner may begin with almost nothing:

> 人

Then:

> 人向山去。

Then a tiny narrative:

> 一个人向山里走。  
> 他没有回头。  
> 山很高。  
> 天快黑了。

Later, the same underlying work can become substantially richer:

- more precise and natural vocabulary
- richer sentence structures
- idioms and culturally meaningful expressions
- additional detail
- dialogue
- character motivations
- another character's perspective
- a different time period
- a side thread that illuminates the main thread
- events that were previously outside the reader's view
- historical and social context
- increasingly literary ways of expressing the same underlying human meaning

The important principle is that the learner is **not being tricked**.

Earlier versions are not secretly "false." They were simply partial.

Later versions should deepen, widen, complicate, or reinterpret the meaning of what came before without casually invalidating it.

---

# 2. What Makes This Different from an Ordinary Graded Reader?

A conventional graded reader normally has:

> Story A → Story B → Story C → harder Story D

JianRu instead explores:

> **Story A₀ → Story A₁ → Story A₂ → Story A₃ → ...**

The same work grows alongside the learner.

However, these are **not merely longer copies**.

An iteration can change one or several dimensions:

### Linguistic depth

The Chinese becomes richer:

- more exact vocabulary
- more natural collocations
- richer syntax
- idioms
- literary language
- culturally meaningful expressions

### Narrative breadth

The learner gains access to material that already existed in the story-world but was previously outside the current narrative window.

### Narrative perspective

The same world may be viewed through:

- the protagonist
- a parent
- a servant
- an antagonist
- a bystander
- an older version of a character
- a child
- a community
- even an anthropomorphised natural element such as a mountain, river, or tree, when artistically justified

### Temporal depth

The narrative can move through time:

- later that day
- a year earlier
- twenty years earlier
- a previous generation
- consequences years later

### Contextual depth

A previously simple event becomes meaningful because the learner now understands the surrounding relationships and circumstances.

### Detail

The same event can be shown in greater richness without turning every increase in difficulty into a mechanical vocabulary exercise.

---

# 3. The Fundamental Invariant: Meaning, Not Wording

The invariant is **not**:

- the exact sentences
- the exact sequence of paragraphs
- a fixed set of semantic facts exposed at every stage
- even one narrowly defined semantic representation

The deeper invariant is the **meaning of the work**.

This allows large changes in the surface representation.

An early version may communicate only the broadest shape:

> A person goes toward a mountain.

A later version may reveal:

- why the person went
- what happened before he left
- what another character believed was happening
- what happened to someone who disappeared
- what the journey means in relation to family, obligation, memory, nature, or social expectations
- how the same event looks from another time or perspective

The learner's interpretation should become deeper rather than merely more fact-complete.

---

# 4. This Is Not a Mystery Box

JianRu should **not** rely on artificial suspense where the reader is encouraged to race through pages to discover a cheap twist.

Avoid the pattern:

> "You thought X happened. Actually, X was false!"

The preferred pattern is:

> "You understood one part of what happened. Now you can understand more of the whole."

Later information should normally **integrate with** earlier information.

A later iteration may reveal:

> what happened to the butler

without implying that the earlier story was deceptive.

The learner should feel:

> "Now I see more of the picture."

not:

> "The author lied to me."

There can eventually be genuinely unreliable perspectives in advanced literature, but this is a sophisticated narrative device and should not be the default mechanism of progression.

---

# 5. The World Comes Before the Individual Iterations

The practical architecture should not be:

> Write the final gigantic story first and mechanically simplify it.

Nor should it be:

> Simulate an enormous universe containing every possible event.

Instead use a staged construction process.

## Stage 1 — Rough Story Idea

Start with a strong, meaningful premise.

At this point it can be rough.

The important question is:

> What is this work fundamentally about?

Not simply its plot, but the human meaning that can support many levels of interpretation.

For example:

> A person's journey toward a mountain gradually becomes a story about family obligation, memory, and what different generations believe they owe each other.

This is a better seed than:

> A man goes to a mountain.

The latter can still be the visible surface of the first iteration, but the author needs to know what larger work exists beneath it.

## Stage 2 — Causal World Skeleton

Expand the rough idea into a coherent skeleton:

- major characters
- important relationships
- major locations
- central conflicts
- significant historical events
- major causes and consequences
- important time periods
- important secrets or unknowns, where appropriate
- major cultural/social context
- potential character arcs
- potential narrative threads

Do **not** attempt to generate every event in the world.

At this stage the goal is a **story-bearing world**, not an exhaustive simulation.

## Stage 3 — Build the World / Event Graph

Represent the meaningful world structure:

- actors
- places
- objects
- relationships
- events
- time
- causes
- consequences
- who knows what
- what each character believes or misunderstands

This is the conceptual "world" from which many valid narratives can be drawn.

The graph can span multiple time scales and connect events that occur far apart.

For example:

> grandfather's action 30 years earlier → father's belief → daughter's decision → protagonist's present journey

The world is the substrate.

It is not itself the finished story.

## Stage 4 — Build a Long Narrative

Once the world skeleton is sound, create a substantial version of the work.

This is where the story's major threads, characters, themes, cultural texture, and literary identity become concrete.

The long narrative is useful as a **creative and editorial reference**, even though it does not need to be the final "complete" representation of the world.

## Stage 5 — Expand the World Where Needed

Once the long narrative exposes gaps or suggests stronger possibilities, fill in the world graph.

A character may need a believable childhood.

A previously minor event may need a cause.

A location may need history.

A relationship may need a backstory.

The world therefore develops recursively:

> rough idea → skeleton → graph → long narrative → graph refinement

This is intentional.

---

# 6. The World Is Not Automatically a Story

A complete event graph could contain thousands of perfectly coherent events.

That does **not** mean every path through the graph belongs in JianRu.

A narrative slice must justify its existence.

Useful contributions include:

### Character

Shows who somebody is or how they change.

### Causality

Explains why an important event happened.

### Relationship

Changes or illuminates a relationship.

### Theme

Expresses or complicates the work's central ideas.

### Cultural meaning

Allows an important social or cultural pattern to emerge through lived behavior.

### Contrast

Shows that another person, generation, or social group understands the same situation differently.

### Consequence

Shows what an earlier choice changed.

### Context

Makes a previously simple event meaningful.

### Symbolic meaning

Deepens an object, place, action, image, or recurring motif.

If a side story contributes none of these, it is probably a distraction.

For example:

> The protagonist meets a fisherman, buys fish, and continues his journey.

If the fisherman has no meaningful role in the parent story, then a full fisherman subplot or fisherman POV is likely harmful.

Beautiful prose is not enough.

**Every expansion must serve the work.**

---

# 7. The JianRu Iteration Engine

The central generation rule is:

> **Given what the learner already knows linguistically and narratively, produce the next version of the same work that provides the best possible learning experience while preserving the integrity of the parent story.**

There are two essential inputs.

## A. Learner knowledge

This comes from the learner's actual SRS / learning state:

- known Hanzi
- known words
- partially known words
- known constructions
- known grammar patterns
- recent exposure
- retention / familiarity
- areas that can now tolerate a small amount of novelty

The learner model should influence what language can be introduced next.

## B. Narrative knowledge

The system must also know what the learner has already encountered in previous iterations:

- which events have been shown
- which characters have been introduced
- which relationships are known
- which parts of the world are already familiar
- which interpretations the learner has already been exposed to
- which narrative threads have already been opened
- which information has intentionally not yet been shown

This prevents later generation from behaving as though the learner is starting from zero every time.

---

# 8. What Can Change Between Iterations?

A new iteration can differ along several dimensions at once.

## 8.1 Better / Richer Language

The same underlying event can later be expressed with:

- more precise vocabulary
- more natural collocations
- richer syntax
- aspectual nuance
- idioms
- discourse markers
- figurative language
- literary phrasing

The new language should be introduced because it improves the story and the learner's Chinese, not because a vocabulary spreadsheet demands another word.

## 8.2 A Different Narrative Thread

The story can temporarily leave the protagonist when the new material meaningfully belongs to the parent work.

Examples:

- the mother's perspective
- the servant's experience
- the antagonist's motives
- the village's perspective
- an event happening elsewhere at the same time

The test is not:

> "Is this an interesting story on its own?"

The test is:

> **"Does this enrich the main work?"**

## 8.3 Another Time Scale

The same place or relationship can be revisited:

- earlier that day
- years earlier
- another generation ago
- years after the central event

A flashback is valuable when it changes the learner's understanding of something meaningful, not merely because flashbacks are dramatic.

## 8.4 More Detail

The exact same narrative can become fuller.

For example:

Early:

> He went home.

Later:

> Why he went home, what he saw on the way, what his mother was doing, what he remembered, and what happened after he arrived.

The added material should remain connected to the story's real structure.

## 8.5 Newly Visible Information

The world may always have contained an event that the learner simply had not yet encountered.

For example:

> The earlier version follows the protagonist and never says what happened to the butler.

Later, a connected thread reveals the butler's fate.

This is legitimate expansion because the event was already part of the world.

---

# 9. The Story Grows by Appending, Not Rewriting the Past

A defining product behavior is:

> **New iterations primarily add narrative material rather than replacing earlier material with harder paraphrases.**

This creates continuity.

The learner should retain access to:

> everything they already read

while gradually encountering:

> what they could not previously understand.

The growth can therefore be:

```text
L0
  one word

L1
  one sentence

L2
  a tiny closed narrative

L3
  the same narrative + meaningful detail

L4
  + another connected event

L5
  + another perspective

L6
  + historical context

L7
  + richer language

...

Advanced
  a large, coherent work exposing a substantial portion
  of the underlying world
```

This is not autocomplete.

The next iteration does not exist merely to predictably continue the previous sentence.

It is a **new narrative realization of the same work**.

---

# 10. A "Story Manifold" Mental Model

A useful conceptual model is a **story manifold**.

The underlying world contains many possible valid narrative paths.

A single story iteration is one path or projection through that world.

```text
                         STORY WORLD
                   space + time + causality
                              |
            +-----------------+-----------------+
            |                 |                 |
          Hero             Daughter         Old Woman
            |                 |                 |
        present           childhood        20 years ago
            |                 |                 |
        mountain          village          same mountain
            |                 |                 |
            +--------- meaningful links -------+
```

The important point is that these paths are not arbitrary.

They must remain anchored to the same work.

A good narrative projection:

- reveals something meaningful
- strengthens the parent work
- fits the current learner level
- creates useful linguistic exposure
- remains coherent with everything previously established

A bad projection is merely an interesting tangent.

---

# 11. The Learner Does Not "Solve" the Story

The intended experience is closer to **progressive understanding** than puzzle solving.

At an early stage the learner may understand:

> A man goes toward a mountain.

At a later stage:

> He is going there because of his family.

Later:

> He is repeating something his father did.

Later:

> His daughter sees his motives differently.

Later:

> The mountain has significance across generations.

Later:

> The learner understands competing interpretations, social pressures, memories, obligations, and consequences.

Nothing needs to say:

> "Here is the true meaning."

The learner's understanding should emerge from accumulation.

---

# 12. Chinese Cultural Depth Must Be Emergent

The aspiration is not merely to make a story:

- set in historical China
- filled with Chinese names
- filled with food, clothing, architecture, and scenery

That is surface-level Chineseness.

JianRu should aim for stories that can become a **window into Chinese cultural and literary traditions** through the way the world is constructed and the way characters live in it.

Potential dimensions include:

- family obligation
- social hierarchy
- reputation
- indirect communication
- restraint
- ritual
- reciprocity
- memory
- relationships between generations
- relationships between individual and community
- attitudes toward nature
- historical consciousness
- culturally embedded taboos
- idiomatic compression
- allusion
- moral ambiguity
- tensions between duty, desire, order, freedom, withdrawal, success, simplicity, etc.

These should **emerge from the story**, not appear as inserted lessons.

Avoid:

> "This story teaches Chinese philosophy."

Prefer:

> the characters behave, speak, choose, avoid, remember, and interpret things in ways that allow the learner to gradually infer culturally meaningful patterns.

Also avoid reducing "Chinese culture" to one unified worldview. Chinese literary history is internally diverse, so different works can draw from different traditions and even contain tensions between them.

---

# 13. The Work Should Contain Meaning Before It Contains Difficulty

A key editorial rule:

> **Do not add material merely because the learner needs another word.**

Bad generation logic:

> "We need to teach five new nouns, so add a market, a fisherman, three vegetables, and a dog."

Better logic:

> "What meaningful part of this work becomes expressible at the learner's new level?"

The language should serve the narrative.

The narrative should serve the work.

The work should serve the learner.

This hierarchy protects JianRu from degenerating into word-list stories.

---

# 14. Vocabulary Is Still Target-Led

JianRu should continue its target-led philosophy.

Vocabulary is not selected from an arbitrary external list and then forced into unrelated stories.

Instead:

> **important stories determine useful vocabulary.**

Then the learner's SRS state determines when additional parts of that language can become accessible.

A new word can be desirable because:

- it is important to the current story
- it is likely to recur later
- it unlocks another meaningful narrative thread
- it belongs to a high-value semantic cluster
- it allows more natural Chinese expression
- it provides a culturally meaningful concept

This preserves the original JianRu idea:

> **build the lexicon backward from worthwhile content.**

---

# 15. The Content Pipeline

A practical end-to-end pipeline is:

```text
1. Rough story idea
        ↓
2. Causal world skeleton
        ↓
3. World / event graph
        ↓
4. Long narrative reference
        ↓
5. Refine and fill the world where needed
        ↓
6. Identify meaningful narrative threads
        ↓
7. Establish the learner's current linguistic state
        ↓
8. Establish what the learner has already seen
        ↓
9. Select the next meaningful narrative expansion
        ↓
10. Realize it in Chinese at the current learner level
        ↓
11. Validate:
      - linguistic appropriateness
      - narrative continuity
      - cultural coherence
      - no irrelevant tangents
      - no unnecessary vocabulary
      - no contradiction with established work
        ↓
12. Append the new iteration to the catalogue
```

This should be an editorial/content-generation system, not just one giant prompt.

---

# 16. The Catalogue Is Part of the Product

Every iteration should remain available.

The learner should be able to revisit earlier states of the work whenever useful.

For example:

```text
Story
 ├── L0
 ├── L1
 ├── L2
 ├── L3
 ├── L4
 ├── L5
 └── ...
```

Earlier versions are valuable for:

- comprehension review
- confidence
- SRS support
- seeing personal progress
- comparing how the same idea was expressed
- returning to a simpler version when needed

The archive therefore becomes a **history of the learner's relationship with the story**.

---

# 17. What We Are Actually Building

The long-term product concept is not:

> "AI generates increasingly difficult stories."

It is:

> **A persistent narrative world that gradually unfolds into increasingly rich Chinese as the learner becomes capable of understanding it.**

The learner begins with almost no access to the world.

Each iteration expands the aperture.

The learner does not simply accumulate vocabulary.

They accumulate:

- language
- narrative knowledge
- cultural context
- relationships
- memory
- interpretations
- literary sensitivity

And these things reinforce one another.

---

# 18. The Ultimate Experience

A successful JianRu story should eventually feel like this:

### Beginning

> 人。

Then:

> 人向山去。

The learner knows almost nothing.

### Early stage

They learn a simple concrete event.

### Middle stage

They discover characters, relationships, motivations, history, and connected events.

### Advanced stage

They can follow multiple perspectives and time periods.

### Very advanced stage

They can understand:

- why a character behaved as they did
- what another character thought about it
- what happened before and after
- how generations understand the same event differently
- which assumptions are cultural
- why a particular idiom or image carries weight
- what the work is saying without the story ever having to explain its own lesson

### End state

The learner has not merely "finished a graded reader."

They have watched **one Chinese work become intelligible to them**.

That is the core vision.

---

# 19. Design Principles to Preserve

1. **The work is one work.**  
   Iterations are different realizations of it.

2. **Later versions deepen rather than casually invalidate earlier versions.**

3. **The invariant is meaning, not wording.**

4. **New material must serve the parent work.**

5. **No artificial mystery-box progression.**

6. **No mechanical "same sentence + new word" progression.**

7. **The learner's SRS state determines linguistic accessibility.**

8. **The learner's prior narrative knowledge determines what can be meaningfully added.**

9. **World construction comes before iteration generation.**

10. **A world is not a story; narrative selection is an editorial act.**

11. **Beautiful prose without meaningful function is not sufficient.**

12. **Cultural depth must emerge through the lived world, not through explicit cultural lectures.**

13. **Do not attempt to encode "all of Chinese" into one artificial work.**

14. **Aim instead for a work that can become a deep, culturally grounded window into Chinese language and literary thought.**

15. **Earlier versions remain accessible forever.**

---

# 20. Working Definition

> **JianRu's expandable-story system is a learner-adaptive narrative architecture in which a coherent Chinese story-world is progressively exposed through increasingly sophisticated and meaningful narrative realizations. Each iteration is generated from the learner's linguistic knowledge and prior narrative knowledge, while remaining faithful to the parent work. Progression may occur through richer language, new information, additional detail, different perspectives, different times, or newly accessible narrative threads. The goal is not to solve a mystery or artificially teach vocabulary, but to let the learner's understanding of one culturally meaningful Chinese work deepen in parallel with their mastery of Chinese.**
